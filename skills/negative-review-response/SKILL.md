# Negative Review Response

Speed and tone decide whether a 1★ costs one customer or fifty. The reply's
audience is every future reader; the reviewer is the second audience.

## Workflow

1. **Get the facts.** `list_reviews` for the location, confirm the exact text,
   rating, age. If hours old — say so; responding same-day reads as a business
   that's awake.
2. **Classify before drafting.**
   - *Legitimate complaint* → full playbook below.
   - *Mixed/unclear* → same playbook, lighter apology, more curiosity.
   - *Suspected fake / policy-violating* (competitor spam, off-topic rant,
     slurs, no customer relationship): draft a short factual reply ("we have no
     record of this visit — contact us at …") AND tell the user to report it in
     Google Business Profile (Reviews → ⋮ → Report). ReputeMap cannot remove
     reviews; never promise removal.
3. **Draft with the 4-step frame** (one short paragraph, 40–90 words):
   acknowledge the specific issue → own it without excuses ("that's not the
   experience we aim for") → move it offline (name + direct contact) → one
   sentence of standing invitation. No arguing, no "but", no customer blame,
   no personal data, no legal threats.
4. **Close the loop.** After the user publishes from the inbox, suggest a
   follow-up check in a week: if the customer updates the review, a short
   grateful edit of the reply is worth drafting.

## Never

Discounts in exchange for editing the review (incentive — prohibited);
copy-paste apologies across different complaints; disputing facts in public —
that conversation belongs offline.
